One Search at a Time

The 2026-08-15 sweep left an open question: when the worker pool triples inference, why does the number of global closure searches stay flat? The answer is that ContradictionAgent holds a single in-flight search slot, and each search is expensive enough to keep it occupied. Under a 16-worker pool it drops 595 closure checks for every one it runs.

The headline

Resolved · both hypotheses were the same one

The agent is time-saturated, and that is precisely why it is trigger-starved. The two explanations the probe was built to separate are not alternatives — one causes the other. A closure search occupies the only search slot for a long time; every check that arrives meanwhile is discarded by the first clause of check_global_closure/1. So searches are simultaneously expensive and rare, and adding workers cannot increase their number.

Under the 16-worker pool, 5 559 732 checks were suppressed as already-in-flight against 9 347 searches actually dispatched. Under the serial pool the same ratio is 81:1.

Three results follow from the run, in descending order of consequence.

  • Parallelism multiplies everything except the answer. The 16-worker pool does 5.8× the inference of the serial pool and searches a frontier 6.5× wider, and converts that into eight more solved problems out of 1 677 — well inside this problem set's own run-to-run churn of about ±35.
  • Roughly half the budget goes into closure search in both configurations. 46.5 % under the pool, 51.6 % serial. The share is large but not pool-sensitive, which is not what either hypothesis predicted; the budget clamps it in both cases.
  • The sweep has a noise floor of about 35 problems, and it was never measured until now. Of 3 354 repeated (configuration, problem) pairs, 84 answered differently across three identical repeats. Any ablation delta smaller than that is not interpretable.

Why the discriminator half-failed

The probe was designed around a prediction: under time-saturation the search's share of wall clock would be large and would grow with the pool; under trigger-starvation it would stay small while the suppressed and skipped counters grew. The measurement returned a third pattern — the share is large in both configurations and slightly smaller under the pool.

Both configurations run about half their total budget inside find_global_closure/3. Saturation is therefore not something the wider frontier causes; it is already present with a single worker. What the pool changes is not how much time the agent spends searching but what it searches: the same clamped budget now covers a space with 6.2× as many candidates.

Two corrections to how that 46.5 % should be read

It understates the cost. csp_duration_us is recorded only on the completion path of the search task (contradiction_agent.ex:493), so the 1 662 searches killed mid-flight under the pool — 17.8 % of all dispatches — contribute zero microseconds to the figure. The true share is higher, and higher by more under the pool, where killing is more common.

It is not stolen from inference. The search runs in its own supervised task, so on a 16-scheduler machine it occupies roughly one core alongside the workers rather than blocking them. Read the share as “about one core-equivalent of the budget went into closure search”, not as “half the proof effort was diverted”. Under worker_pool_size: 1 this is the more striking reading: the machine is running one worker and one closure search, and the search is the larger consumer.

About a third of engaged runs spend 80 % or more of their budget inside closure search and solve almost nothing. This is a correlation, and a confounded one — hard problems produce both large search spaces and unsolved conjectures, so the table cannot show that the search caused the failure. What it does establish is that the population exists and is large: on roughly 1 500 runs per configuration, the proof attempt is in practice a closure search that does not finish, not a tableau expansion.

The noise floor

Three repeats per pair were requested to give the ablation chapter a stability estimate it did not have. The result bears directly on how the 2026-08-15 sweep can be read.

Affects the existing sweep's conclusions

Of 3 354 repeated (configuration, problem) pairs, 84 (2.5 %) did not give the same answer across three identical runs. Per configuration, 49 problems under the pool and 35 under the serial pool solve in some repeats and not others.

The sweep reports baseline at 747 solved and serial at 746 across all 5 109 problems. That one-problem difference is noise, and so is the eight-problem difference measured here. Single-run ablation deltas below roughly ±35 problems on this problem set carry no signal.

The instability is inherent to the design rather than a defect: workers race, the agent's evidence arrives in nondeterministic order, and the 2 000 ms budget cuts wherever it happens to fall. Any configuration comparison in the chapter needs either repeats or a delta large enough to clear that band. The contradiction-agent ablation itself clears it comfortably — no_contradiction_agent solves 381 against the baseline's 747 — so the agent's value is not in question here. Its scheduling is.

What this suggests changing

The agent is load-bearing: removing it halves the solve count. The problem is not that it searches, but that it searches once, at length, and discards the several hundred opportunities that arrive while it is busy. Three directions follow from the numbers, cheapest first.

  1. Bound the search instead of the session. The longest single search is 1 982 ms of a 2 000 ms budget, and 17.8 % of dispatches are killed unfinished. A per-search deadline — a fraction of the remaining budget rather than the session deadline currently passed as deadline_us — would convert one hopeless search into many cheap ones. The 12.4 % success rate suggests most of what is abandoned was not going to close anyway.
  2. Let the in-flight check queue rather than vanish. Suppression currently loses information: the evidence that triggered the check is gone by the time the slot frees. Keeping the most recent suppressed trigger and running it on completion would cost one struct field and turn 595 discarded opportunities into one taken one.
  3. Reconsider whether one slot is the right number. The single slot exists to bound cost, but the pooled configuration leaves 15 schedulers to the workers while one searches. Allowing a small number of concurrent searches over disjoint branch subsets is the change most likely to make the wider pool worth its inference — which, on this evidence, it currently is not.

The measurement to repeat after any of these: this same probe, which now runs unattended in under five hours and reports all three counters.

Method and reproduction

The problem set is every TPTP problem on which either the baseline or the serial configuration of the 2026-08-15 sweep dispatched at least one closure search — 1 677 of 5 109. Problems the agent never engages carry no signal for the question and were excluded. Both configurations run on a single parse of each problem, and their order alternates per problem and repeat so that warm-up cannot load onto whichever runs first.

The one-line source change this run depends on

Checks suppressed because a search was already in flight were not recorded by any counter — the first clause of check_global_closure/1 returned without incrementing anything. That path is the central finding above, so the probe was run with it instrumented:

// lib/shot_tx/prover/contradiction_agent.ex:428
  defp check_global_closure(%__MODULE__{pending_search: %Task{}} = state) do
+   Stats.incr(state.ets_tables, :csp_calls_inflight_suppressed)
    {:noreply, state}
  end

The change is uncommitted, so the git_sha recorded in ca_probe.meta describes the tree minus this line. Without it the csp_inflight_suppressed column reads 0 throughout and the finding is invisible: the sweep's own CSVs export neither this counter nor csp_calls_skipped, csp_duration_us, csp_branches_count, csp_max_domain_size or csp_total_candidates.
